The HomeView felt like something different for Apple – a family friendly device that could take me away from the screens of my other devices and could engage with primarily by voice. I often use an iPad in the kitchen while cooking, so this felt like a natural device to slot in. I also have been wanting something that my kids can use to call me, FaceTime grandparents, and check the calendar – this finally felt right!

Does it stack up? My expectations were high. Here's what I thought:

### First impressions

The HomeView is _tiny_. The 7-inch display is almost comically small. If I had opted for the wall-mounted version, it would be about the same size as my Lennox S30 thermostat. I unboxed it over lunch and – like all new Apple devices lately – I had to wait for a software update. 

I went with Space Grey, and it looks very sharp. Screen and base included, it is about the same footprint of my Sonos One speakers, albeit a touch wider. The base feels solid and I wasn't worried that it would tip over. The articulating mount wasn't too tight but stayed in place even with solid jabs.

I returned later and completed the setup. It automatically setup via my iPhone and the process was very smooth. Now to put it to the test.

### As a kitchen device

I love to cook. I prefer Crouton on iOS and on the iPad. There's nothing like this (yet) for the HomeView. The only way you can get any semblance of a recipe like Apple's promotional materials is by opening up a website and asking Siri to make it in to a recipe. No Safari action, no separate app. Just Siri. This was a particular challenge for me, as most of the recipes I have are my own, and cannot be found on the web.

My best workaround for this was to privately publish my recipes on my website, then visit those in the browser. A fair bit more work. Once Crouton or Mela have apps designed for HomeView, I imagine this will help.

When I'm cooking, I like to move around the kitchen. With the HomeView, you're anchored to wherever it's plugged in. With a device this small, I would expect there to be a battery built-in so I could quickly relocate it, but there isn't. 

### For music

I'm a Spotify user, so I expected some rough edges here. The experience was surprisingly smooth. I asked Siri to play some music, and it rightly defaulted to Spotify. The Spotify app appeared on the HomeView home screen, and I was able to browse my library that way. As far as I was able to tell, it was really just playing audio by AirPlay from my iPhone and was not connecting to Spotify directly. Overall, the experience was quite corollary to using Spotify with CarPlay.

Compared to my older Sonos One, I don't think the audio held up. It sounds okay, but did not really offer room-filling audio. For a more contained audio experience, it is workable.

### The family device I've been waiting for?

Admittedly, this was what I was the most excited for. I've been eyeing the Skylight and Hearth for some time, and when this came out I thought it would be perfect.

It is unequivocally not. 

One of the first things I tried was setting up profiles for my kids. They do not have their own devices (precisely something I'm hoping to avoid) but they do have Apple IDs. All profiles are controlled not by your Apple ID "Family" settings, but rather by your Home sharing settings (HomeKit). To add a user, you must invite them to your home. 

The big caveat here is that **they need a device**. I had an older iPad and iPhone lying around, so I signed them in and attempted to invite them. This never worked. The error message I got was unclear (or it would just fail silently), but it seems that the likely issues was due to older iOS - I believe the device needs to be on iOS 27 or later – not an option for these old devices. Even if it did work, this is a workaround that goes against the whole point of the HomeView for me – allowing the kids to use it without them having their own device.

### Controlling your smart home

This is where the HomeView shines. All of my home devices are HomeKit enabled - Lutron switches for lights, UniFi Protect cameras, HVAC, etc. The controls for these are easily accessible, and the HomeKit automatically shows the camera views contextually when people are spotted, the doorbell is ringing, and other "urgent" alerts as required.

Unfortunately for the HomeView, all of the other devices in the Apple ecosystem already do just as good of a job of this. 

### The verdict

I sent it back. It fell short of my expectations, but my expectations were also for a device that they didn't really promise – and that's on me.

The HomeView truly feels like an extra large Apple Watch. Even using Safari evokes the minimal and difficult to use browsing experience of the Watch. I would describe using it as a hybrid between the Apple Watch and CarPlay. Constrained in ways that the device format should allow breaking out of.

 I look forward to a future "HomeView Max" that hopefully redeems some of these shortcomings. 

If you want a smart thermostat that looks great and has the added bonus of a few other features, get the wall-mounted version. Otherwise, it's a pass for me.
